Evidence

Experiences are a starting point.

Owner-submitted stories can help us ask better questions about access to care. They are not a representative survey of all pet owners.

Evidence status: 10 November 2026

Why the charts are paused.

The earlier charts combined historical and current information, and different questions used different counting bases. We are checking duplicate handling, publication permissions and small groups before releasing figures again.

We have removed the headline counts and percentages from these pages. A larger submission total alone would not resolve those checks.

Read the dated correction and next steps

What this evidence can help us investigate.

  • When an owner was asked to pay, and what they understood about the amount.
  • How insurance, referrals and support options were explained.
  • Which parts of the process people found helpful or difficult.
  • Which practical changes deserve testing with veterinary teams and insurers.

Owner accounts are experiences reported to the project. They are not independent assessments of clinical decisions, invoices or professional conduct.

What it cannot establish.

People choose to respond, often because they have faced a difficult experience. The sample cannot tell us the proportion of all owners affected, prove that a provider overcharged, or establish that a financial barrier caused a particular medical outcome.

UK and US experiences need separate analysis. Professional contributions are also a separate source, and the current professional sample is too small for public percentage claims.

Before any new figures are published.

  1. Check which submissions are eligible for public use and how repeat submissions are handled.
  2. Separate countries and explain any differences between older and newer questions.
  3. State what every count and percentage measures, including any themes coded from written answers, and give its denominator.
  4. Apply a documented rule for small groups and include the date, limitations and method with the results.

We can publish useful questions and independently sourced context while that work happens. We do not need to wait for an arbitrary story-count milestone.

Independent context.

The UK Competition and Markets Authority’s veterinary market investigation has produced reforms on information and transparency, with requirements taking effect in stages. Our work should examine how emergency payment and referral processes work in practice alongside those reforms.

RCVS guidance on communication and consent is also relevant to explaining treatment options and costs. These sources do not validate our own dataset or make UK rules applicable in the US.
