Our plan

Make the next step towards care clearer.

We are starting in the UK with four questions to investigate with owners, veterinary teams, insurers and welfare organisations. These are proposed areas of work, not reforms already secured.

Four questions worth testing.

1. Can the financial explanation be easier to use?

Once it is clinically safe to discuss options, can an owner see the treatment estimate, the amount due now, what might change, who can explain it and when it will be reviewed?

Who can help: emergency and referral hospitals, veterinary teams and practice groups.

2. Can insurance payment decisions be clearer?

Can the hospital and insurer explain whether a direct claim is possible, what approval is needed, how long a decision may take and what the owner must pay? Cover, excesses and limits still matter.

Who can help: insurers, claims teams and veterinary hospitals.

3. Can the financial handover follow the referral?

Where an urgent referral is needed, can the receiving hospital explain expected payment requirements and insurance arrangements before the next decision, without delaying necessary care?

Who can help: referring practices, specialist hospitals and insurers.

4. Can support routes be more honest and useful?

Can owners be told who a support option is for, what it may cover and how quickly it can respond? Payment options need clear costs and suitability checks; fundraising cannot be treated as guaranteed emergency cash.

Who can help: animal welfare organisations, hospitals and responsible payment providers.

Start with a workable test.

The aim is to develop one small test with willing organisations, then publish what it changes. Useful measures might include understanding of costs, time to a payment decision, the cash an owner must find upfront and staff workload.

Clearer paperwork alone is not proof that more animals received treatment. Any access claim would need evidence that supports it.

Veterinary care has real staffing, equipment and clinical costs. The professionals providing it need to help shape solutions.

Now, next and later.

  1. Now: check the evidence, explain its limits and prepare a focused UK briefing.
  2. Next: seek feedback on the four questions and explore one feasible test with willing organisations.
  3. Later: report results, develop a separate US approach and explore a properly governed way to amplify individual fundraisers.

No partner, test or emergency support service has been agreed. Progress will be reported through dated updates.

US supporters still have a part to play.

You can contribute an experience from your own country, follow the UK work and help us reach relevant researchers and organisations. We will keep US evidence separate and build any US proposals around the systems that apply there.
